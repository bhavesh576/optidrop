Why Track Body Measurements?

The scale does not tell the whole story. Body measurements provide a much more accurate picture of your physical progress, especially when you are building muscle and losing fat simultaneously. While your weight might stay the same, your waist could shrink and your arms could grow — that is positive body recomposition.

Tips for Accurate Measurements

  • Measure at the same time of day, ideally in the morning before eating
  • Use a flexible, non-stretchable tape measure
  • Keep the tape snug but not tight against your skin
  • Measure each area at its widest or most prominent point
  • For arms and thighs, measure the same side each time (typically the right)
  • Take 2-3 measurements and use the average for best accuracy

What the Trend Indicators Mean

Each measurement shows an up or down arrow compared to your previous entry. Green indicates a decrease (positive for waist/hips), while red indicates an increase. For biceps and chest, an increase is often a positive sign of muscle growth. Context matters — consider your goals when interpreting trends.

Frequently Asked Questions

The most common measurements to track are chest, waist, hips, biceps, and thighs. These five measurements give a comprehensive picture of your body composition changes. Measure at the same time of day and under similar conditions for consistency.
All your measurement data is stored locally in your browser using localStorage. Nothing is sent to any server, making it 100% private. However, clearing your browser data will delete your measurements, so we recommend exporting your data regularly.
For best results, measure yourself every 2-4 weeks. Measure at the same time of day (ideally morning), under the same conditions. Weekly measurements can show too much natural fluctuation, while monthly may miss trends. Consistency in measurement technique is key.
Trend arrows show whether each measurement is increasing or decreasing compared to your previous entry. This helps you quickly see if your training and nutrition plan is working. Downward arrows on waist measurements and upward arrows on biceps typically indicate positive body recomposition.
