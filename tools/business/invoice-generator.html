Frequently Asked Questions

A professional invoice should include your business name and contact details, the client's name and address, a unique invoice number, the invoice date and due date, a detailed list of products or services with quantities and unit prices, the subtotal, applicable tax rate and amount, and the total amount due. Payment terms and accepted payment methods should also be specified.
Invoice numbers should be unique and sequential. Common formats include simple numbering (INV-001, INV-002), date-based numbering (INV-2024-001), or client-based numbering (ACME-001). The key is consistency — choose a format and stick with it. Never reuse an invoice number, as this helps with bookkeeping and tax records.
The due date depends on the payment terms you set. Common terms include Net 30 (due 30 days from invoice date), Net 15 (due in 15 days), Net 60, or Due on Receipt. For freelancers, Net 15 or Net 30 is most common. Always clearly state the due date on your invoice to avoid payment delays.
An invoice is a bill sent to a client requesting payment for goods or services — it is issued before payment is made. A receipt is proof of payment that is issued after payment has been received. Invoices are used for accounting and record-keeping before transactions are complete, while receipts confirm that a transaction has been finalized.
