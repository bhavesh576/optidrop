Frequently Asked Questions

An experience letter is an official document issued by an employer to a departing employee. It certifies the employee's tenure, designation, department, roles, responsibilities, and performance during their employment. It is commonly required when applying for new jobs, visa applications, or higher education.
A comprehensive experience letter should include the employee's full name, employee ID, designation, department, date of joining, last working date, a description of roles and responsibilities, key achievements, reporting manager details, and a statement of good conduct.
An experience letter details the employee's tenure, roles, responsibilities, and performance during their employment period. A relieving letter simply confirms that the employee has been officially relieved from their duties and is free to leave. The experience letter focuses on what the employee did; the relieving letter focuses on the formal exit process.
While not legally mandated in most jurisdictions, experience letters are considered standard HR practice. Most new employers require an experience letter from the previous company to verify employment history and assess the candidate's background.
