Frequently Asked Questions

An experience letter (also called an experience certificate) is an official document issued by an employer to a departing or former employee. It certifies that the employee worked at the organization and includes details such as their job title, department, duration of employment, and a brief assessment of their conduct and performance. It serves as proof of work experience for future employment.
An experience letter should include: the company name and address, employee's full name, employee's designation/job title, department, date of joining and last working day, a brief description of roles and responsibilities, a conduct and performance assessment, and the signature of the authorized person with company stamp. The letter should be on company letterhead for authenticity.
While often used interchangeably, there is a subtle difference. An experience letter details the employee's tenure, role, and performance during their time at the company. A relieving letter specifically confirms that the employee has been relieved from their duties and has completed all formalities. Some companies issue both separately, while others combine them into a single document.
To get an experience letter: submit a formal written request to your HR department or manager before your last working day, follow up if you don't receive it within a reasonable timeframe, check if your company has a standard process for issuing experience letters, and ensure all exit formalities (knowledge transfer, equipment return, clearance) are completed. Most companies issue it on or before the last working day.
