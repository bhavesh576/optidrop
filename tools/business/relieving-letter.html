Why is a Relieving Letter Important?

Most employers require a relieving letter from the previous company as proof of clean exit. It protects both the employer and the employee by documenting the end of the employment relationship, the last working date, and confirming that all handover procedures have been completed.

Frequently Asked Questions

A relieving letter is an official document issued by an employer to an employee who is leaving the organization. It confirms that the employee has been relieved from their duties and responsibilities effective from a specified date. It serves as proof that the employee has properly resigned and completed all formalities.
A relieving letter should be issued on or before the employee's last working day. It is typically handed over after the employee completes the notice period, returns company assets, and finishes all handover formalities. Most companies issue it on the last working day itself.
While there is no universal legal mandate, most organizations issue relieving letters as a standard HR practice. Many new employers require a relieving letter from the previous company as proof of employment history. Not providing one can create difficulties for the employee in their future career.
A relieving letter confirms that the employee has been officially relieved from their duties and is free to leave the organization. An experience letter details the employee's tenure, roles, responsibilities, and performance during their employment. Some companies combine both into a single document, while others issue them separately.
