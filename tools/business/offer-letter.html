Frequently Asked Questions

A job offer letter should include the job title, start date, work location, salary and compensation details, benefits (health insurance, retirement plans, PTO), probation period, reporting manager, work schedule, and any conditions of employment (background check, reference check). It should also include the offer expiration date and instructions for accepting the offer.
An offer letter is generally considered a preliminary document and may or may not be legally binding depending on the jurisdiction and specific language used. In most cases, it is an expression of intent rather than a binding contract. However, certain terms within the offer letter (like compensation for a specified period) may be enforceable. It is advisable to consult with a legal professional for specific guidance.
Yes, it is perfectly acceptable and common to negotiate after receiving an offer letter. You can negotiate salary, benefits, start date, work arrangement (remote/hybrid), vacation days, signing bonus, and other terms. The best approach is to express enthusiasm for the role while professionally presenting your counter-offer with supporting reasons.
An offer letter is issued to a candidate to propose employment terms before they join. It is a formal invitation to work at the company. An appointment letter is issued after the candidate accepts the offer and formally joins the organization. The appointment letter contains more detailed terms of employment including detailed responsibilities, reporting structure, code of conduct, and other HR policies.
