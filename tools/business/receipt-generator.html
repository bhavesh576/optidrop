Frequently Asked Questions

A receipt is proof of payment — it is issued after the customer has paid. An invoice is a request for payment — it is issued before the customer pays. Receipts confirm that a transaction has been completed, while invoices are used to request and track pending payments. Both are important for bookkeeping and tax records.
A proper receipt should include: the business name and contact information, the customer name, the date of payment, a unique receipt number, a list of items or services purchased with amounts, the subtotal, any applicable tax, the total amount paid, and the payment method. Including these details makes the receipt professional and useful for both parties' records.
To create a receipt for a cash payment, include all the same details as any receipt: your business name, the customer name, date, receipt number, items purchased, amounts, and total. Write 'Cash' as the payment method. If possible, have both parties sign the receipt. A digital receipt generated with a tool like OptiDrop's Receipt Generator works perfectly for cash transactions too.
In most jurisdictions, businesses are legally required to provide receipts for transactions, especially for goods sold or services rendered. The specific requirements vary by country and state. For example, in many places, businesses must provide receipts for transactions above a certain amount. Even when not legally required, providing receipts is a best practice for professionalism, dispute resolution, and tax compliance.
