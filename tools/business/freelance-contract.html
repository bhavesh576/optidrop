Frequently Asked Questions

Yes, a contract is essential for freelancers. It protects both parties by clearly defining the scope of work, payment terms, deadlines, and intellectual property rights. Without a contract, disputes over payment, deliverables, or timelines can be difficult to resolve. A written agreement also adds professionalism and builds trust with clients.
A freelance contract should include: names and contact details of both parties, a detailed project description and scope of work, start and end dates, total compensation and payment schedule, number of revision rounds included, intellectual property ownership terms, confidentiality/NDA clauses, termination conditions, and the governing law jurisdiction.
In many jurisdictions, verbal agreements can be legally binding. However, they are extremely difficult to prove in court. Written contracts are always recommended because they provide clear evidence of what was agreed upon. For freelance work, always use a written contract to protect yourself from non-payment or scope disputes.
Scope creep occurs when a client requests additional work beyond the original agreement. To handle it: define the scope clearly in your contract, specify the number of revision rounds included, include a clause stating that additional work will be billed separately, and communicate changes in writing before starting extra work. A good contract is your best defense against scope creep.
