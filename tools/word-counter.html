Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is the word counter?

The word counter is highly accurate for standard English text. It splits words on whitespace boundaries, meaning any sequence of characters separated by spaces, tabs, or line breaks counts as one word. This matches the counting method used by most word processors like Microsoft Word and Google Docs. For languages that do not use spaces between words (such as Chinese or Japanese), the count may differ from specialized tools. The sentence counter detects sentences by looking for periods, exclamation marks, and question marks.

Is there a character or word limit for this tool?

There is no strict character or word limit imposed by the tool itself. You can paste extremely long documents — even full book chapters — and the counter will process them instantly. The only limitation is your browser's memory, which for modern browsers comfortably handles hundreds of thousands of words. If you notice any slowdown with very large texts, try pasting in smaller sections. The tool processes everything locally, so there is no server-side restriction on text length.

How is the estimated reading time calculated?

The reading time is calculated using an average reading speed of 200 words per minute, which is the widely accepted standard for adult readers of English text. This means a 1,000-word article would show an estimated reading time of 5 minutes. For texts under one minute, the tool displays the time in seconds. Keep in mind that actual reading speed varies based on the complexity of the content, the reader's familiarity with the topic, and whether the text contains technical jargon or visual elements that require attention.

Is my text sent to any server or stored anywhere?

No. Your text is never uploaded, transmitted, or stored on any server. All counting and analysis happens entirely within your web browser using JavaScript. When you close the page or clear the text area, your content is gone permanently. This makes the tool safe to use with confidential documents, unpublished manuscripts, business proposals, and any other sensitive text.
