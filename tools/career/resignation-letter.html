Frequently Asked Questions

The standard notice period is two weeks, but this can vary depending on your employment contract, company policy, and your role. Senior positions may require 30 days or more. Always check your employment agreement and give as much notice as possible to maintain a positive relationship with your employer.
A resignation letter should include a clear statement of resignation, your last working day, a brief expression of gratitude for the opportunities you had, and an offer to help with the transition. Keep it professional, positive, and concise. There is no need to explain your reasons for leaving in detail.
You are not obligated to explain why you are leaving in a resignation letter. If you choose to include a reason, keep it brief and positive, such as pursuing a new opportunity or focusing on career growth. Avoid negative comments about the company, colleagues, or management. The resignation letter becomes part of your employment record.
To resign professionally, schedule a face-to-face meeting with your manager before submitting your written resignation letter. Give adequate notice, offer to help train your replacement or document your responsibilities, and maintain a positive attitude during your remaining time. Express gratitude and avoid burning bridges, as professional relationships are valuable throughout your career.
